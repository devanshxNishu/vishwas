The three acts

Tap an act to hold it. In act C, tap a class to isolate it — blue is the model right, fire is the model wrong.

All 1,252 gridpoints, drawn from the model's own output. Truth: IMD 0.25° gauge-gridded daily rainfall, colocated onto the 0.5° GEFS grid. Bust = |ensemble mean − observed| above the 95th percentile for that region and lead time, fitted on 2018 training data only.

The product, Day 1 to Day 10

Confidence is scored per gridpoint, per lead day.

Not one number for a region — 1,252 separate verdicts, re-scored for every lead day. Day 5 is the model's own output for 5 August 2019, and so is every other day on the bar. Step out through the horizon and watch confidence drain: the wet regimes go first, and by Day 10 central India and the Northeast are where a forecaster should stop trusting the number.

The console

The part a forecaster
actually opens.

Everything above argues the case. This is the instrument: a duty forecaster picks a date and a lead day, reads which gridpoints not to trust, and gets the reason in words. Dense, quiet, one screen, no scrolling.

Open the console→

Prototype on archived 2019 reforecast dates, not a live feed. It flags forecasts likely to fail — it never corrects a forecast, and it shows no observed rainfall.

01 · SELECT Six reforecast dates, ten lead days, one click each. Every lead day independently measured.
02 · SEE India on the half-degree grid in three named states. Zoom and pan to a district.
03 · EXPLAIN Click a flagged gridpoint and its top drivers read out in plain English, with percentiles.
04 · TRIAGE The ten worst areas, ranked and clickable, plus an alert budget that answers "how many alerts is that?"
05 · REPORTS ITS OWN SKILL Move the lead-day control and the measured skill at that horizon moves with it. A confidence system that states its confidence at the horizon you are looking at, rather than asking to be trusted uniformly.
